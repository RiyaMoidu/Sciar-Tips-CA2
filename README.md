Part 4a: Theory Behind the Build

Leadership Theory – Servant Leadership
Greenleaf’s (1970) servant leadership theory emphasises listening, employee empowerment and prioritising employee needs. Aoife’s statement, “Owners pay us, staff don't” (04-aoife.md), reflects the tension between commercial interests and employee welfare. Rafael’s exclusion from tip calculations for hours worked after 23:00 highlights the importance of recognising the contributions of less powerful employees. However, servant leadership does not fully address the commercial pressures faced by Sciar.

Organisational Culture – Psychological Safety
Edmondson’s (1999) concept of psychological safety explains the importance of employees feeling safe to express concerns. Rafael’s statement, “please dont say it was me who ask” (01-rafael.md), indicates a fear of potential consequences. This challenges Niamh’s assumption that the absence of complaints indicates employee satisfaction.

Influence on Design
These concepts influenced my decision to disclose tip calculations, provide accessible financial information and introduce a concern-reporting mechanism, enabling informed employee participation.

Competitive Advantage and Limitations
Financial transparency and employee voice could differentiate Sciar from competitors by strengthening trust, credibility and stakeholder relationships. However, transparency alone does not guarantee equitable distribution, and psychological safety requires supportive management practices beyond technological solutions.

Part 4b: Decision Record
Decision 1 – Refusing a Stakeholder Request
Aoife and Declan requested that the tip distribution rule remain hidden (04-aoife.md; 02-declan.md), while Niamh opposed a concern-reporting mechanism (03-niamh.md). Rafael’s reluctance to raise concerns highlights existing communication barriers. Based on fairness, inclusion and accountability, I prioritised financial transparency and employee voice while protecting individual earnings. Withholding information is not an effective leadership strategy. Clear financial communication can reduce disputes and strengthen trust, addressing management’s concerns. Rafael’s excluded working hours also highlight the need to reconsider shift allocation and tip distribution, if necessary. Evidence of privacy risks would encourage me to reconsider how information is presented.

Decision 2 – Rejecting an AI Suggestion
AI initially recommended bilingual functionality considering Rafael’s Portuguese-speaking background. However, his message indicated a basic understanding of English. I prioritised accessible plain English over bilingual functionality because the application’s primary objective was to improve employees’ understanding of financial information. My decision was based on relevance, usability and stakeholder needs. Evidence from user testing identifying language barriers would encourage me to reconsider bilingual functionality.

Personal Bias – Automation Bias
Initially, I trusted AI-generated webpages without sufficiently verifying their functionality. However, testing revealed errors in tip navigation and the employer dashboard. Multiple corrective instructions were required, as evidenced in my AI sessions. This helped me recognise the importance of independently testing AI-generated outputs rather than assuming their accuracy and functionality.
